# Eclipsing Eden Book Art Style Guide

## Purpose

This document defines the **visual rendering style** for Eclipsing Eden book art.

It is not a scene guide, lore guide, prompt library, or list of preferred subjects. Its job is to help future generations reproduce the look and feel of the art style without biasing the image toward any specific weather, era, location, race, faction, monster, vehicle, architecture, or action scene.

## Prime Directive: Style, Not Subject Matter

Do **not** infer content from this guide.

This guide does not imply a default:

- era
- location
- weather
- season
- time of day
- biome
- culture
- race or species
- faction
- profession
- monster
- vehicle
- weapon
- prop
- scene type
- action type

Those details must come from the user's prompt or from a separate content brief.

When using this guide, separate the prompt into two parts:

1. **Subject brief:** what the image depicts.
2. **Style brief:** how the image should look.

This document only supplies the style brief.

## Core Style Statement

Eclipsing Eden book art should use:

**dark, moody, full-color painterly realism with subdued color, grounded material detail, historical texture, and matter-of-fact supernatural integration.**

The style should feel serious, atmospheric, and physically believable. Fantasy elements should look like they belong in the world, not like decorative overlays.

## Relationship to Branding

The established Eclipsing Eden branding is built from:

- dark charcoal and black foundations
- aged gold and antique brass linework
- bone or parchment highlights
- restrained blood-red accents
- occasional verdigris
- occult Art Deco structure
- Gothic ecclesiastical weight
- sacred geometry and celestial symbolism
- industrial relics and forbidden-scripture mood

For full-color art, translate these into tone and rendering rather than copying them literally.

Use the branding influence as:

- restrained contrast
- serious occult mood
- antique metallic accents
- sacred or ritualized compositional weight
- subtle geometry where appropriate
- a sense of myth, industry, sin, faith, and age

Do not automatically add eclipses, halos, diagrams, borders, blood, churches, bullets, circuitry, or ornament unless requested by the subject brief.

## Rendering

Use:

- painterly realism
- visible texture
- naturalistic forms
- believable anatomy
- tangible materials
- readable silhouettes
- sourcebook-quality polish
- subdued detail rather than hyperactive detail

Avoid:

- glossy digital concept-art sheen
- photorealistic camera-still rendering
- anime or manga styling
- cartoon stylization
- comic-book inking
- plastic skin
- pin-up presentation
- over-smoothed AI rendering
- literal old-oil-painting filters that blur or muddy the image

The desired result is not “make it look like an old painting.” It is better described as:

**modern painterly realism with restrained historical-fantasy mood.**

## Color

Use muted, restrained full color.

Preferred color families include charcoal, slate, smoke gray, wet stone gray, dark navy, muted brown, weathered leather, dull olive, moss green, oxidized copper, aged brass, antique gold, bone white, parchment beige, deep dried-blood red, and ember orange used sparingly.

The palette should feel worn, grounded, and atmospheric.

Avoid high-saturation fantasy color, candy-colored magic, bright heroic color grading, clean modern gradients, neon color unless specifically requested, rainbow spell effects, and excessive color pop.

## Light and Atmosphere

Favor mood, depth, and physical cohesion.

Lighting should usually feel restrained, integrated with the environment, and dramatic without becoming theatrical or glossy.

Atmosphere may include rain, mist, smoke, dust, ash, fog, glare, haze, or clear air, but only when the subject brief calls for it or when it naturally supports the requested scene.

Do not default to any atmospheric condition.

## Materials

Materials should feel touchable and specific.

Emphasize believable treatment of cloth, leather, metal, brass, wood, stone, paper, bone, mud, dust, water, smoke, fire, skin, scales, fur, machinery, religious objects, and occult objects.

The viewer should feel that objects have weight, age, texture, and use.

## Historical and Cultural Grounding

When a prompt specifies an era, region, profession, culture, or technology level, represent it with care.

Ground the image through clothing silhouette, tailoring, tools, weapons, vehicles, architecture, signage, social posture, material wear, and practical gear.

Do not substitute generic fantasy costume or generic modern equipment when a specific historical or cultural context is requested.

If no historical context is specified, do not invent one from this guide.

## Supernatural Integration

Supernatural elements should feel physically present.

Good supernatural rendering:

- interacts with light and shadow
- affects nearby matter
- obeys the image's atmosphere
- has visual structure
- feels like part of the same world as the mundane objects
- is impressive without overwhelming the image

Avoid generic video-game spell blasts, excessive floating runes, pasted-on magic circles, disconnected glow effects, and spectacle that erases the grounded mood.

Use occult geometry, sacred light, infernal color, or divine radiance only when it fits the subject brief.

## Composition

Prefer clear, readable compositions with strong visual hierarchy.

A good image should have a clear focal subject, understandable supporting forms, readable action or mood, believable depth, purposeful negative space or detail density, and sourcebook-scale clarity.

The composition may be quiet, dramatic, intimate, violent, ceremonial, pastoral, urban, cosmic, domestic, or anything else the subject brief requires.

Do not make every image an action scene. Do not make every image a character portrait. Do not make every image dark weather or occult architecture.

## Character Treatment

Characters should feel grounded in their world.

Use practical clothing when appropriate, believable gear, expressive but restrained faces, natural body language, culturally specific detail when requested, and era-appropriate silhouettes when requested.

Avoid pin-up posing, sexualization by default, exaggerated fantasy armor by default, fashion-shoot energy, plastic skin, and generic fantasy outfits where specificity is needed.

Attractiveness is allowed. The key is restraint, taste, and character credibility.

## Prompt Style Block

Use this reusable style block after a subject brief:

> Render as a full-color Eclipsing Eden book illustration in dark, moody painterly realism. Use subdued color, grounded material detail, restrained lighting, and atmospheric depth. Make all clothing, objects, architecture, creatures, and supernatural effects feel physically present and believable within the requested scene. Use antique brass, aged gold, bone white, deep red, or ember accents only where they naturally fit. Avoid high-saturation fantasy color, glossy concept-art sheen, anime or comic styling, pin-up presentation, plastic skin, and generic fantasy clichés.

## Negative Guidance

Use this when correcting drift:

> Do not add unrequested weather, locations, eras, monsters, vehicles, factions, props, or recurring motifs from prior examples. Avoid bright saturated colors, generic heroic fantasy, glossy digital surfaces, anime/cartoon/comic styling, excessive magical glow, floating decorative runes, pin-up posing, and modern design elements unless requested. Keep the image painterly, grounded, subdued, and faithful to the subject brief.

## Acceptance Checklist

Before accepting an image, ask:

- Does the image follow the requested subject brief?
- Did it avoid importing unrequested content from prior examples?
- Does it feel like Eclipsing Eden rather than generic fantasy?
- Is the color restrained?
- Is the rendering painterly rather than glossy?
- Are materials believable?
- Are supernatural elements integrated into the scene?
- Is the composition readable?
- Are character designs grounded and tasteful?
- Does the image avoid anime, comic, pin-up, plastic, and high-saturation drift?

## Common Failure Modes

### Subject contamination

Problem: the image drifts toward previously successful scenes or motifs.

Fix: restate that the style guide does not imply subject matter, and that all content must come from the current subject brief.

### Over-saturation

Problem: the image becomes bright modern fantasy art.

Fix: reinforce muted full color, subdued palette, and restrained highlights.

### Glossy concept art

Problem: the image looks like polished video-game key art.

Fix: emphasize painterly realism, material texture, and reduced sheen.

### Literal antique painting filter

Problem: the image becomes muddy, blurry, or faux-historical.

Fix: ask for modern painterly realism with historical mood, not a literal old oil painting.

### Decorative magic

Problem: supernatural effects appear as pasted-on symbols or generic spell graphics.

Fix: require physical interaction, restrained glow, and integration with the environment.

### Generic fantasy design

Problem: clothing, armor, weapons, or architecture become vague fantasy defaults.

Fix: specify the era, culture, profession, or material logic in the subject brief.

## Final Principle

This guide defines a **flexible house rendering style**.

It should help many different Eclipsing Eden subjects feel visually coherent without forcing them into the same weather, era, locations, races, monsters, or scene types.

The style is: **subdued, painterly, grounded, haunted, material, and serious.**
